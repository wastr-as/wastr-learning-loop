# Lens: partner (operator or hauler)

> Type-level frame. Hypotheses drawn from early operator conversations, not from any one stakeholder.
> Refine from real notes; a stakeholder's own folder always outranks this file.

## Who this is

A small or mid-size collection operator that already runs its own routing, job and invoicing tools,
and decides whether WASTR adds jobs or only adds administration.

## What they decide

Whether to accept WASTR-originated jobs, under which commercial terms, and what job data they share.

## How they judge WASTR

- **Incremental, profitable jobs** with little added administration beat replacement software.
- **Customer control:** they keep the customer relationship, pricing authority and invoicing.
- **Job-scoped data:** only what is needed to execute one job; no historical or secondary use.
- **Job economics first:** no fee or commission without concrete contribution examples.
- **Capacity:** demand, not tooling, is often the constraint; capacity is checked per job.

## Evidence they trust

Concrete job examples with price, effort and risk; a small trial with their own tools; references
from operators like them. Not: dashboards, projections, platform claims.

## Typical objections

"We already have this built." "Who owns the customer?" "What do you do with our data?" "What does it
cost us in time per job?" "Why would we need a new platform?"

## Must not say or imply

That WASTR sets their prices, owns their customers, or may reuse their data. Avoid "platform for
everyone" framing; the neutral-platform claim must not read as favouring one operator.

## Vocabulary

Oppdrag, sekk, container, henting, kapasitet, fakturering. Use their words, not product jargon.
